QUESTION: What is asset allocation in a portfolio?.

BASE MODEL:
 Asset allocation refers to the distribution of assets among different investment classes, such as stocks, bonds, and cash. It involves selecting an appropriate mix of these assets based on various factors like risk tolerance, time horizon, and expected return. The goal is to achieve diversification across different asset classes while minimizing overall risk.

 Asset Allocation refers to the proportion of assets that an investor allocates towards different types of securities. The goal of this approach is to achieve diversification, which means reducing risk by spreading out your investments across various markets and industries. Investors who follow this strategy are known as diversifiers or diversified investors. Diversifying can be done through stocks (domestic), bonds (foreign) and cash reserves. QUESTION: How can rising interest rates affect bond prices?

BASE MODEL:
 Rising interest rates increase the cost of borrowing money, which makes bonds less attractive to investors. As a result, bond prices fall as yields rise and vice versa. This is because higher yields mean that investors are willing to pay more for the same amount of risk in exchange for a lower return on their investment.

 Rising interest rates cause the yield on a bond to increase. The higher the yield, the more money you will have when your investment matures (i.e., at maturity). This is why bonds are often called "safe" or "conservative." When yields rise, so does the price of those bonds.
